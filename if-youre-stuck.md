# If you're stuck

Quick fixes for the things that stop people in the first ten minutes.

## I don't have a talk idea

You don't need a great idea. You need a real one. Pick the first of these you can answer in one sentence:

- **The thing you'd tell a new hire on day one** that isn't in the docs.
- **A mistake you made** and what you'd do differently.
- **A tool you switched to or from,** and why.
- **Something you've explained to a colleague three times.** If they needed it, so does a room.
- **A bug that took more than a day.** That's an Incident talk.
- **Something everyone on your team believes that you think is wrong.** That's Myth vs Reality.

Still nothing? Use your work from last week. "What I learned doing ___ last week" is a valid talk for this lab. You can swap topics later. The templates work the same way.

## I don't have a CFP accepted

Most people in the room don't. You're writing the talk you'd submit. When you're done, your Segment 02 sentence is the last line of your abstract, and your three boxes are its outline. You'll leave with most of a CFP submission.

## I'm not comfortable sharing

That's fine. Every share in this lab has a quieter option:

- **Neighbor shares:** you can tell your neighbor "I'll pass on this one."
- **Room shares** happen all at once. Say it under your breath. Nobody can tell.
- **The hook wall** is anonymous.
- **The hot seat** starts at rung 1, reading your hook from your seat. You can also stay in the audience and give feedback, which is practice too.

If you want a lower-stakes first step, read your hook to one host after the session.

## My talk doesn't fit any frame

Pick the closest one and bend it. Two common cases:

- **A talk that compares options** ("Postgres vs. DynamoDB for X"): use Myth vs Reality. The myth is "you should always pick ___."
- **A talk that's a list of tips:** use the Zoom Lens. "Why it matters" is the cost of not knowing the tips. Order the tips from simplest to deepest, and end with where they stop working.

Box 1 still has to name the stakes.

## The wifi is down

- **A page you already have open keeps working,** and your fill-in boxes still save on your phone without a connection.
- **Can't load the site at all?** Use paper. The templates are short enough to write on an index card. The hosts will read them out.
- **Hook wall down?** Everyone writes their hook on an index card, folds it, and passes it to the front. Hosts read 5 or 6 aloud. Same exercise, still anonymous.
- **Hosts:** the [cut line](hosts.md#the-cut-line) doesn't change. Run the lab from the projector.

## I'm running out of time in a segment

Leave the box half-filled and keep going. You need a rough draft of the opening (Segment 04) more than you need a perfect story arc. Come back to the rest after the session. Your answers will still be there.
