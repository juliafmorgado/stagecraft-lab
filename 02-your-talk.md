# Your talk

A talk isn't about a topic. It's about what changes for the people in the room. Spend five minutes deciding who your audience is and what they can do when they leave.

## Find your topic · 1 min

If you picked one on the [overview](index.md#pick-your-topic-now), skip this. If not, answer the first one that gets a reaction from you:

- What are you working on right now?
- What did you learn the hard way?
- What have you explained to a colleague three times?
- What broke?
- **Have a CFP accepted?** Paste the abstract into the topic box on the overview and use that talk.

## Who is in the room? · 1 min

Be specific. "Developers" is too broad. Try "backend engineers who've just been handed on-call," or "platform teams choosing between two tools."

Ask yourself:

- What do they already know? What can you skip?
- What are they worried about, or stuck on?
- Why would they pick your talk over the one next door?

## What do they walk away with? · 2 min

Write one sentence. If you can't, the talk isn't ready yet, and that's fine. This is the step that fixes it.

> This talk is about [topic] for [target audience] so that when they leave, they can [primary takeaway].
{: .card data-label="Template card"}

> The takeaway must start with a verb the audience can **do** or **decide**: choose, debug, set up, replace, measure, ask, stop. "Understand," "learn about," and "be aware of" don't count, because you can't see someone do them.
{: .rule}

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| ...so they **understand** observability. | ...so they can **find** the slow service in a trace in under a minute. |
| ...so they **learn about** feature flags. | ...so they can **decide** which flags to delete this sprint. |
| ...so they're **aware of** supply chain risk. | ...so they can **set up** dependency pinning before Friday. |
