# Imposter syndrome

Nearly everyone who gives technical talks feels like an imposter at first. You're not going to eliminate that feeling in seven minutes. You're going to speak it aloud, then turn it into your strength.

## Host stories · 1 min

Each host tells a short, true story about a time they felt like a fraud on stage or before a talk, and what they did anyway. Keep it honest and under 30 seconds each.

## Say it to a neighbor · 2 min

Turn to the person next to you. Pick **one** stem, finish it out loud, then swap. No fixing, no advice. Just listen.

> - "My biggest fear about speaking on stage is ___."
> - "The thing I'm afraid someone will notice is ___."
> - "Something I know that most of the room doesn't is ___."
> - "The last time a talk went well, it was because ___."

## Reframe it · 2 min

Now turn the fear into a reason you belong on that stage. You're not denying the fear, you're adding the half of the sentence you usually leave out.

> I'm not [X], but I am [Y], and the room needs that because [Z].
{: .card data-label="Template card"}

A few to get you started:

- "I'm not a Kubernetes maintainer, but I am the person who migrated 40 services to it last year, and the room needs that because most of them are about to do the same."
- "I'm not a native English speaker, but I am someone who had to learn this stack from scratch, and the room needs that because I remember which parts were confusing."
- "I'm not the most senior engineer here, but I am the one who was on call when it broke, and the room needs that because nobody else saw it from the inside."

{% include field.html id="reframe" placeholder="I'm not ___, but I am ___, and the room needs that because ___." %}

> **X** is the thing you think disqualifies you. **Y** is something true about you that's specific, not generic: "someone who was there" beats "passionate." **Z** is about the audience, not you.
{: .tip}
