# Nerves

Nerves don't go away with experience. Experienced speakers just have a routine for them. You'll leave this segment with yours.

Your racing heart and shaky hands are the same body response as excitement. You can't switch it off, but you can give it somewhere to go.

## The day before and 5 minutes before

Most stage panic comes from surprises. Remove the surprises you can.

#### The day before

- [ ] Run your opening out loud once, standing up, at full volume.
- [ ] Check your slides on the laptop you'll actually present from, with the real adapter.
- [ ] Have a backup: slides as a PDF on a USB stick or in the cloud, and a recording or screenshots of any demo.

#### 5 minutes before

- [ ] Check the mic: clip it on your collar, not your chest, and say one sentence at talk volume.
- [ ] Click through two slides with the clicker.
- [ ] Stand on your mark in the empty room and look at the back row.
- [ ] Water within reach. Phone on silent.

## Together, right now · 4 min

Do these as a room. The hosts lead, everyone follows.

1. **Fist clench and release.** Squeeze both fists as hard as you can for 5 seconds, then let go completely. Three times. Tensing a muscle on purpose makes it easier to let it relax afterwards.
2. **Box breathing, 4 rounds.** Breathe in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. The host counts out loud. A slow exhale helps slow your heart rate.
3. **Anchor phrase.** Say one short sentence out loud that reminds you why you're up there. Your Segment 01 reframe works. So does "They came to learn, not to judge" or "I know this better than anyone in the room."
{: .steps}

> **4 in · 4 hold · 4 out · 4 hold**
>
> Repeat four times. If 4 seconds feels too long, use 3.
{: .card data-label="Box breathing"}

Now write your own pre-talk ritual: two or three things you'll do in the last five minutes before you go on.

## On stage

- **Go slow on the first sentence.** You know your hook by heart, so say it at half speed. Nerves speed you up, and the room needs a second to settle anyway.
- **If you're shaky:** plant your feet hip-width apart and press your big toes into the floor. Let your hands rest at your sides or hold the clicker loosely.
- **If your mind goes blank:** pause → take a slow breath → look at the slide title → say the slide's key point in your own words. The slide title is your cue. That's what it's there for.
- **If the demo fails:** say what you expected to happen ("At this point, you'd see the trace appear") and switch to your backup screenshot or recording. The room doesn't mind a broken demo. They do mind watching you debug it for three minutes.
- **If you lose your place:** "Let me back up one step" is a complete sentence. Nobody minds.

> A 3-second pause feels long to you and normal to the room. Most of the time, the audience doesn't notice a pause at all, or reads it as confidence.
{: .tip}
