# The opening

The room decides in thirty seconds whether to give you their attention or open their laptop. You don't need charisma. You just need to jump straight into the story.

## Pick a hook · 2 min

Pick one pattern and fill it in with something true from your talk.

- **A moment**
  <span class="arc">"We almost lost ___ when ___."</span>
  Drop the room into the scene, then rewind. Works with the Incident and Build Story frames.
- **A real number**
  <span class="arc">"[Number] [unit]. That's how long / how many ___."</span>
  "Four hundred and twelve. That's how many times we got paged in March." Use a number from your own system or a source you can cite. Never round a guess into a statistic.
- **Show of hands**
  <span class="arc">"Raise your hand if you've ever ___."</span>
  Pick something most of the room has done, so most hands go up. Raise yours too.
- **A screenshot or meme**
  <span class="arc">One image the room recognizes instantly.</span>
  A terminal error, a 2 a.m. Slack message, an all-red dashboard. Say nothing for 3 seconds, then: "This was my Tuesday." Use your own screenshots or ones you have rights to.
- **A confession**
  <span class="arc">"I broke production in my second week."</span>
  Admitting a mistake early makes the room trust you, and tells them this won't be a sales pitch.
- **A bold claim**
  <span class="arc">"Most dashboards are lying to you."</span>
  Strong, short, and slightly uncomfortable. You must pay it off later in the talk, or you lose the room's trust.

## The 60-second formula · 3 min

> [Hook line] + [Who you are, in one sentence] + [What the room gets, in one sentence]
{: .card data-label="60-second formula"}

**Who you are** isn't your job title and résumé. It's why *you* are the one telling this story: "I'm the engineer who got those 412 pages."

**What the room gets** is your Segment 02 takeaway, said to them directly: "In the next 25 minutes, I'll show you how we got that down to 30, and how you can do it with the alerts you already have."

{% include field.html id="opening" rows=5 placeholder="Hook. Who you are. What the room gets." %}

Now say it under your breath, start to finish, and time it on your phone. Over 60 seconds? Cut words from the middle sentence first.

### Don't open with

- **"Hi, my name is..."** Your name is on the slide and in the schedule. Say it after the hook, if at all.
- **An agenda slide.** It tells the room the talk hasn't started yet. If you need one, put it after the opening.
- **An apology.** "Sorry, I'm a bit nervous" or "I didn't have much time to prepare" asks the room to lower their expectations. They will.

## The closing line · 1 min

The last thing you say is what people remember and repeat. Pick one pattern:

1. **Callback to the hook.** "Four hundred and twelve pages. Last month we had nine."
2. **The takeaway, in one sentence, again.** Repeat your Segment 02 verb so it's the last thing they hear.
3. **Monday morning, do this.** "On Monday, open your alert list and delete one rule nobody has acted on in 90 days."

> Never end with "Thanks, any questions?" Say your closing line, pause, *then* say thank you. If you want to do Q&A you can let the room clap, and then transition: "I'll take a few questions now."
