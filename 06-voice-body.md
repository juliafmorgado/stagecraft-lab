# Voice and body

The same sentence can sound like a guess or a fact. The difference is in how you say it and where you stand. You'll practise both on your climax line.

## Vocal micro-drills · 3 min

Pull up your climax line from [Segment 03](03-story-arc.md#the-tension-curve). Murmur it to yourself three ways. Not performed, just quietly enough that you can hear the difference.

1. **Pitch drop: authority.** Let your voice go down on the last word, like you're closing a door. Statements that rise at the end sound like questions.
2. **Whisper: focus.** Say it much quieter than the rest of the talk. A room leans in when a speaker gets quiet, as long as the mic can still pick you up.
3. **3-second pause: tension.** Pause for a full three seconds *before* the line. Count it in your head. Then say it.
{: .steps}

> Which one felt most like you? That's the one you use over your climax slide. Use the other two for the rest of the talk.
{: .tip}

## Stage geometry · 3 min

Where you stand is part of the story. Pick spots on purpose so you aren't drifting.

{% include stage-map.svg %}

- **Home base** is next to the podium or laptop. Explain, set up context, and run demos from here.
- **The climax X** is front and center. Walk there for your climax line and stand still. Feet hip-width apart, weight even on both feet. Don't move again until you've said it.
- **Walk on transitions, plant on points.** Move while you switch topics. Stop while you make a point. Pacing during an important sentence distracts from it.
- **Eye contact in three zones.** Pick one person in the left, center, and right sections. Give each one a whole sentence before moving on. Don't sweep across the room like a lighthouse.
- **Stay out of the projector beam.** If the slide is on your face, take a step to the side.

## Body checklist · 2 min

Stand up and try each one. These help most people. If one feels wrong for your body or your talk, skip it.

- **Hands:** let them move naturally between your waist and shoulders. If you don't know what to do with them, hold the clicker loosely in one hand and let the other rest.
- **Volume:** talk to the back row. If the person farthest away can hear you, everyone can.
- **Pace:** slow down for numbers, names, and anything you want written down.
- **Filler words:** when you feel an "um" coming, close your mouth and pause instead. A pause sounds deliberate. An "um" doesn't.
- **Face:** look at the room when you finish a sentence, not at the screen.
